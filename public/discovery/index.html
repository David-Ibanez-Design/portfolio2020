Transforming Sales Engagement: A User-Centered Approach to Improving Lead Interaction in Digima

  • Introduction
  • Generating and selecting solutions
  • Testing assumptions
  • Conclusion
Digima is a web-based CRM application that helps small to medium real estate companies understand their customers' needs. Sales personnel struggled to identify leads likely to result in meetings, causing missed opportunities and leading users to question our product’s ability to deliver results.

01 | Problems

  • Sales staff couldn't tell which leads were worth contacting next, causing missed opportunities and inconsistent follow-up
  • Existing notifications were noisy and undifferentiated, so users were ignoring them
  • Without a reliable way to prioritize outreach, customer confidence in Digima's ability to drive results was eroding

02 | Goals

Evaluate a new home screen with a "Hot lead" list ranking contacts by meeting potential, against three independent assumptions:

  • Feasibility: could we generate the list reliably from existing data?
  • Usability: would users notice it, understand it, and act on it?
  • Desirability: would they adopt it as a habit, or treat it as another ignored notification?

03 | Role and responsibilities

  • Co-led the product discovery workshops with PM and engineering, including the OST and assumption prioritization
  • Ran moderated usability sessions with five participants, and designed the Meeting Potential rating system that fixed the comprehension issues they surfaced
  • Instrumented a live prototype rollout with funnel measurement against pre-registered success thresholds, which also validated the Meeting Potential fix at scale
  • UI/UX design across V1 and V2 of the dashboard

04 | Outcomes

  • Feasibility and usability validated. The desirability test returned the most valuable result: strong initial usage that did not sustain — exposing the real bottleneck
  • The rollout confirmed two deeper problems the feature couldn't fix: profile pages too dense to scan at the moment of action, and thin lead lists pointing to the content of outreach — not the identification of leads — as the true constraint
  • That finding became the direct starting point for the team's next major initiative: the AI Prospecting Agent

Introduction

Insight gathered during discovery interviews showed that salespeople struggle to identify high-potential leads and craft engaging messages, leading to missed opportunities and unresponsive outreach.

During our product discovery process, we identified 2 opportunities that we wanted to focus on. The first one was as follows:

"It is often difficult for salespeople to identify which leads they should reach out to/the ones with a high potential of obtaining a meeting in the short term."

Here are some customer quotes taken directly from interviews that show the issue:

  • “I often don't know what action I should take for a customer, and frankly, I don't have the time to do it either.”
  • “Because long-term customers keep their original sales rep, when they reply to an automated email, sometimes the rep doesn't notice or report it, meaning we can't catch those missed follow-ups. We often don't realize that outreach has been omitted.”
  • “I only reach out to customers who are in the middle-to-long term segment when I have spare time. I don't schedule it; I just do it when I think, 'I have time now, so I might as well do it”

The second one is addressed in a separate case study. To address these opportunities, I organized a collaborative workshop where the team and I brainstormed potential solutions.

Generating and selecting solutions

I led a workshop with the team to prioritize solutions based on their potential impact and associated risks, ultimately selecting the creation of a new home screen in Digima to enhance customer engagement.

The team members voted on the ideas that had the highest chance of addressing each opportunity. Each member had 3 votes; the following 3 solutions were selected:

  • Improve the current notification system. Eliminate bulk email notifications and allow users to receive only truly essential alerts. (Destination: Email remains; existing chat tools or LINE Mobile require verification)
  • Create a new home screen in Digima displaying customer lists and to-do lists, enabling immediate identification of unattended customers
  • Suggest content outreach ideas to customers.

We selected a single idea by surfacing assumptions attached to these ideas and rating them based on how much impact they will have on the success of the idea, the level of risks attached to the assumption if the assumption were to be proven wrong, and how much evidence we already have that the assumption is true. The ideas with the most number of assumptions that carry the most risks if proven wrong and the ones that also have the least amount of evidence, should be prioritized.

Using this system, we selected the following idea:

  • Create a new home screen in Digima displaying customer lists and to-do lists, enabling immediate identification of unattended customers

And the following assumptions were selected for testing:

  • Can we provide a list of leads likely to respond to user contact attempts?
  • Will users act on the recommended leads by taking a significant action?
  • When presented with a list of mid-to-long-term customers they should reach out to in Digima, will users regularly use the feature?

Testing assumptions

Testing the three assumptions in sequence validated feasibility and usability, and returned a mixed but decisive answer on desirability.

An important part of the product discovery process is to test selected assumptions using small experiments that require minimal effort.

Assumption 1: Is it technically possible to provide a list of leads likely to respond to user contact attempts? (Feasibility)

This assumption addressed the technical feasibility necessary for the solution's core value proposition. This required an internal investigation led by the engineering side of our product trio (PM, design, engineering).

The engineering team conducted a small-scale proof-of-concept (POC) using the existing Digima database. The goal was to quickly determine if the necessary data existed to create a real-time list based on predictive analytics of response likelihood.

Technical constraints and performance metrics (time required to generate the list, data reliability) were defined as acceptable technical benchmarks prior to the investigation.

The investigation confirmed that sufficient data existed (or could be sourced) and that the predictive logic could be integrated without requiring a complete re-architecture of Digima (Feasibility assumption validated). If the data infrastructure had proven inadequate, we would have revised the solution to rely on technically simpler criteria.

Assumption 2: Will users act on the recommended content by taking a significant action? (Usability & Behavior)

This assumption tested the immediate usability and actionability of the proposed interface and content. We needed to verify that the design effectively guided users to perform the desired "significant action" (e.g., initiating contact/setting a meeting).

Using the simple prototype showing a ranked lead list of contacts. I conducted a series of moderated user testing sessions. Participants were given a specific task: "It is time for your next outreach session. Using the page, identify the best lead to reach out to right now and initiate contact".

By conducting this test, I wanted to evaluate/understand the following:

  • Users notice the new contact list
  • Users understand the meaning and goals of the list and what kind of contacts are displayed on the list
  • Users are able to select a specific contact from the list
  • What criteria do users use to select customers in the list?
  • Users are able to perform a significant action, or mention that they could perform an action on the selected contact
  • What influences a user's decision to contact or not contact a customer on the customer detail page?

We defined success quantitatively: at least 80% of participants needed to successfully initiate contact with a recommended lead.

The test showed some flaws with the design of the prototype. For example, users had difficulties understanding the list’s purpose and what kind of contacts were displayed in the list. To help solve that issue, I introduced a “Meeting potential“ rating system, which displayed how likely a lead was to respond positively to the user’s contact attempt, reinforcing the purpose of the list and at the same time, helping the user decide which contact they should reach out to first.

Once the success metrics showed that most users were able to initiate contact with a recommended lead, it was the signal that we needed to decide to move on to testing the last main assumption.

Assumption 3: Will users regularly re-engage mid-to-long-term customers in Digima if we provide a solution for it? (Desirability & Habit)

This assumption addressed the fundamental desirability of the solution: whether the new functionality was valuable enough to prompt a shift in salesperson behavior and establish a sustained habit of re-engagement.

To test this opportunity, we introduced the previously tested list of “Hot leads“ as a new element on the dashboard page as a live prototype. The prototype was only made available to a small and diverse number of accounts and users that were selected based on characteristics such a account industry, user job title, and usage habits.

The goal of this simulation was to observe if and how often salespeople spontaneously incorporated checking the "re-engagement list" into their daily routine over a set period, but also to take that opportunity to gather quantitative data analyzed as part of a funnel, such as:

  • How many users use the list function?
  • How many users select a contact from the contact list?
  • How many users perform a significant action on a contact?

We established the success threshold usage frequency and the percentage of users reaching certain funnel steps upfront to ensure the team was aligned on interpreting the results and to guard against confirmation bias.

By tracking usage in the live prototype, we gathered early signals on whether the solution was being integrated into users' workflows and whether they were performing significant actions on the surfaced contacts. The results were mixed: initial usage was promising, with most users noticing the list and clicking into it, but funnel completion was lower than we'd hoped, as fewer users than expected took meaningful follow-up action. Furthermore, a lack of continued re-engagement with the list became a noticeable issue over time. This aligned with feedback from our discovery phase, where users had mentioned lacking the time, resources, and motivation to compose dedicated outreach messages for their leads. Combined with the structural issues surfaced during rollout (described in the conclusion below), these behavioral signals and user constraints are what shifted our team's attention from list-building to content-generation, ultimately becoming the catalyst for the AI Prospecting Agent project.

Conclusion

The experiment yielded mixed but highly valuable results. While the new "Hot lead" list successfully captured users' attention, with most of the test cohort clicking into it during the observation window, it ultimately fell short of establishing a sustained habit of re-engagement.

The rollout surfaced a more fundamental barrier we hadn't anticipated: the bottleneck wasn't just finding the right leads, it was contacting them. Users simply lacked the time, resources, and motivation to draft dedicated outreach messages.

Next Steps

This realization shifted our product focus. We learned that to truly solve the engagement problem, we needed to empower salespeople to overcome these constraints so they could actively generate content, rather than just tracking a few “Hot leads.”
