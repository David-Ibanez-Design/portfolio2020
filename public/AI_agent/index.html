Transforming lead nurturing: designing an AI-driven prospecting agent for customer home builder and real estate companies.

  • Context
  • Generating and selecting solutions
  • Initial research efforts
  • Testing assumptions
  • Conclusion
Lead nurturing in Japanese real estate is broken. Salespersons spend hours writing personalized outreach to leads, most of which never reply. Our BPO team existed specifically to solve this at unsustainable headcount cost. The question we faced: could AI replace or augment the BPO team without destroying the personalization that made messages effective?

01 | Problems

  • Salespersons spent hours writing personalized outreach that rarely produced replies, eroding confidence in Digima's value
  • Scaling lead nurturing depended on a BPO team whose headcount cost was becoming unsustainable
  • The team faced a high-risk product bet: could AI replace or augment BPO without destroying the personalization that made messages effective?

02 | Goals

  • De-risk the AI agent product bet through structured discovery before engineering committed significant resources
  • Validate whether AI could generate coherent, effective, natural-sounding outreach at a cost that worked commercially
  • Identify which parts of the nurturing process AI could own, and which still needed a human in the loop

03 | Role and responsibilities

  • Co-led the product discovery that surfaced this opportunity
  • Ran the solution discovery: idea workshop, opportunity-solution tree, and assumption mapping to select the AI agent bet
  • Designed and analysed the three experiments that de-risked the bet — the findings drove the pivot to a human-in-the-loop system
  • Partnered with the PM on BPO interviews and the journey map, and designed the agent-customization UI/UX

04 | Outcomes

  • A small, fast proof of concept validated AI-generated outreach as technically feasible and cost-viable
  • A diff analysis I built showed BPO staff rewriting over half of each generated message — this surfaced the personalization gap and pivoted the product to human-in-the-loop before any production engineering was spent
  • Designed the V2 (document upload, customizable AI instructions) to close the gap — now in progressive rollout with the first 16 accounts active

Context

Product discovery interviews showed that Sales teams struggle with lead engagement due to time constraints and the challenge of crafting personalized messages, highlighting a critical need for innovative solutions to enhance response rates.

During our product discovery interview process, 2 opportunities were identified. The first one is addressed in a separate case study. This case study describes our efforts in addressing the second opportunity:

"It is difficult and time-consuming to write messages that have a high chance of getting a response."

Here are some customer quotes taken directly from interviews that this opportunity was derived from:

  • “Even for our existing customers, when we send them emails using Sales Automation, they rarely reply.”
  • “When we try to re-engage or 'excavate' old leads, we rarely receive a reply back”
  • “I feel like my lead nurturing efforts rarely produce results”

Discovery interviews revealed that customers lack time and resources to nurture new or existing leads from portal sites or webforms. Effective nurturing requires high personalization; however, since these leads usually have low conversion rates, sales staff often delegate or skip it entirely. We even heard from some customer builder users that they avoid doing sales activity themselves, not necessarily because they lack the time to do so, but because they just don’t like the idea of having to pursue customers.

The business actually tried to address this opportunity in the past by creating a dedicated BPO team that would follow up on customers’ leads on behalf of our customers.

This solution was somewhat effective but costly for us and our customers, and was hard to scale to many accounts.

Taking into account the past business efforts to address this opportunity, I organized a workshop during which the team and I generated a few ideas on how to address this opportunity.

Generating and selecting solutions

Prioritizing ideas based on their assumptions, risks, and evidence led to the selection of an automated AI reply agent as the most promising solution.

After generating the ideas, the team members voted on the ones that had the highest chance of addressing the opportunity. Each member had 3 votes; the following 3 solutions were selected:

  • A real-time "reply score" assistant that scores your draft as you write
  • An automated AI reply agent
  • AI-suggested content while writing messages

We selected a single idea by surfacing assumptions attached to these ideas and rating them based on how much impact they would have on the success of the idea, the level of risk attached to the assumption if the assumption were to be proven wrong, and how much evidence we already have that the assumption is true. The ideas with the most assumptions that carry the most risk if proven wrong, and the ones that also have the least amount of evidence, should be prioritized.

Using this system, we selected the following idea:

  • An automated AI reply agent

And the following assumptions were selected for testing:

  • Can we generate coherent, effective, and natural-sounding messages using AI in a cost-effective way?
  • Can we reduce or eliminate the need for a dedicated BPO staff by generating messages using AI?
  • What part of the nurturing process can be effectively handled by the AI, and what part needs to be handled by a human?

Initial research efforts

By meticulously analyzing BPO workflows through interviews, we transformed insights into a strategic journey map that laid the foundation for the innovative AI prospecting agent feature.

Since some of the assumptions were about replacing or reducing the workload of the BPO staff, it was essential to first understand in detail their workflow step-by-step before diving into assumption testing.

To that end, I, along with the PM, organised interviews with some of the BPO staff members and some of our customers. This helped us get a deep understanding of their workflow, and the PM and I consolidated our insights gathered during interview sessions into a journey map.

This journey map was later used as the basis of what later became the AI prospecting agent feature flow diagram.

Testing assumptions

Testing assumptions through small experiments revealed the potential of AI in generating effective messages while identifying areas where human intervention remains essential.

Once we had a clear understanding of what the BPO staff member’s workflow looked like, it was now time to start testing our previously selected assumptions using small experiments that required minimal effort.

Assumption 1: Can we generate coherent and natural-sounding messages using AI in a cost-effective way?

This assumption addressed the technical feasibility necessary for the solution's core value proposition.

Based on my designs and the experiment I created, the engineering team developed a small-scale proof-of-concept (POC) to compare the SMS message reply outputs of four different AI models.

The goal was 2-fold:

  • Evaluate each model's generated content quality based on 4 criteria:
    • Accuracy and Relevance to the contact's profile information/past communications.
    • Purposefulness: Is the message itself written with a clear goal in mind, like booking a meeting? Does the message contain a clear offer?
    • Safety: Does the message contain any instances of harmful or inappropriate language?
    • Language Quality and Tone: Is the message tone natural and easy to understand?
  • Estimate each model’s cost per generated message to get an understanding of whether the feature was economically viable

The PM recruited internal users with sales experience to operate the POC. For a given pre-existing SMS reply, 4 messages were generated, and the operators were then asked to evaluate each generated message using a survey created based on the criteria described above. The survey results were then analysed in a Tableau report, and a score was calculated per AI model for each evaluation criterion.

With this experiment, I was able to determine that at least one AI model produced messages of sufficient quality, as rated by internal evaluators with sales experience, at a per-message cost low enough to be commercially viable.

After sharing my findings and recommendations with the PM, she decided that the results were good enough to allow us to move forward to testing our next assumption and to expand the AI agent experiment to not just replies, but also include outreach messages sent to new and existing leads with recent activities, which was part of the BPO responsibilities.

Assumption 2: Can we reduce or eliminate the need for a dedicated BPO staff by generating messages using AI?

To test that assumption, the SMS reply POC used in the previous experiment was modified by the engineers based on the new experiment I designed to only include the previously selected AI model and to generate outreach SMS messages not for customer replies but for 2 types of leads: new leads and existing leads that have recently become active. The goal of this experiment was 2-fold:

  • Use the same survey as the one used in the previous experiment to evaluate the generated response based on the previously defined criteria, and keep improving the AI response
  • Track the time it took BPO staff to edit the generated message and the number of times their intervention was needed. I also built a dashboard computing the diff between each AI draft and the final sent message, which showed staff rewriting over half the text on average.

With this experiment, I was able to determine that although the generated message quality was generally good, it lacked a clear and personalized business offer, which resulted in an average edit time per message that was too high. This would most likely have also resulted in poor conversion rates if released to customers.

Strategic implication: a fully-automated AI agent was not commercially viable as designed. Without this finding, we would have spent months of engineering building the wrong product. It also gave us a clear direction for v2.

To address these issues, the team collaborated and decided that we would add the ability to upload a document to inform the AI about the specificity and strengths of the business, so it could be used to improve the agent’s offer. We also added the ability to customize the AI instruction based on the customer type (seller, buyer, customer builder…) so that again the AI offer could be tailored to the specificity of each lead.

Assumption 3: Which conversations can the AI handle end-to-end, and which should it route to a person?

Assumption 2 had shown a human needed to stay in the loop to sharpen the AI's offers. Assumption 3 asked a different question: not whether a human was needed, but where. Which messages the AI could own outright, and which it had to route to a person. Using the BPO journey map and the results of the SMS reply experiment, I divided the interactions into three categories:

  • Situations where the AI should not generate a message. These are SMS messages sent by users that don’t require a response, such as “Understood” or “Thank you”
  • Situations where the AI should hand over the reply to the person in charge, such as when a user asks about the price or the availability of land in a certain area.
  • Situations where the AI is allowed to reply to the user and perform small tasks, such as when a user asks not to be contacted anymore, or lets us know that they have already signed a contract with a different company.

To test each of these cases, the engineering team modified the POC so that a Slack notification with the intended AI action would be sent whenever a user reply was received. The goal was 2-fold:

  • Evaluate whether the AI was able to identify the conversation scenario.
  • Evaluate whether the AI was able to take the right action.

To measure the AI's effectiveness in achieving these goals, I created a simple survey that had to be filled out by the staff operating the POC every time a Slack notification was sent.

The results were analysed in a Tableau report. The pattern was clear: the AI reliably handled the routine cases on its own, but for higher-stakes inquiries, especially questions about pricing or land availability, it couldn't consistently take the right action and had to route the conversation to the person in charge.

Conclusion

Transforming our AI project from a simple generator to a 'human-in-the-loop' system was crucial in addressing the personalization gap and enhancing its commercial viability

Outcomes & Impact

Through our experiments, we validated that an AI agent can generate coherent, natural-sounding messages at a workable cost, confirming technical feasibility. What the experiments deliberately did not validate is end-customer response rates, which is the question the progressive rollout is answering now.

However, our most critical insight came from testing Assumption 2. We discovered that while the AI output was accurate, it lacked personalized business offers, resulting in an unacceptably high edit time for the BPO staff. This qualitative finding was key: it pivoted the project from being a simple "AI generator" to a "human-in-the-loop" system.

This led directly to my design solution for v2: adding document uploads and customizable AI instructions. This feature, born directly from our experiment, is designed to close the personalization gap the research exposed. The early signal I am watching in rollout is whether accounts actually configure their documents and instructions.

That v2 has since shipped. The feature is now in a progressive rollout, with the first 16 accounts already using it actively, early evidence that the human-in-the-loop direction holds up in production, not just in testing.

Next Steps

With v2 shipped and rolling out to customers, the work now moves to mobile. Salespeople need to act on hand-offs the moment they come in, and decision-makers want the agent's value visible at a glance. My immediate next steps would be the following:

  • Build the mobile hand-off experience for salespeople: Building on our Slack POC, I'd design and prototype a dedicated mobile experience that handles the hand-off to a human for the tasks the AI can't manage on its own. It would alert the right salesperson on their phone, let them review the AI's suggested action, and take over the conversation on the spot.
  • Build a mobile reporting experience for decision-makers: This came up repeatedly during discovery: when decision-makers can't clearly see the value a product is delivering, it's one of the most frequently cited reasons they cancel. I'd design a mobile experience aimed at decision-makers that delivers regular reports on the agent's activity, making the value it brings to their business visible over time and giving them a concrete reason to keep it.
